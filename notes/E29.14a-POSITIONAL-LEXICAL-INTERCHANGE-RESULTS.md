## Registered verdict

All six independently scored claims passed:

1. the complete layer-0 MLP update almost exactly moved the base prompt to the
   clean donor endpoint;
2. restricting that update to positions whose token identities differ retained
   the effect, while shared/static positions did almost nothing;
3. the matched layer-0 attention update did almost nothing;
4. the same intervention worked in reverse, from donor to base;
5. reverse lexical localization also passed; and
6. graded interpolation rose monotonically from the clean recipient to the full
   interchange in the aggregate and in the registered minimum of 10/12
   contexts.

The registered interpretation is therefore a component-specific,
bidirectional lexical-state interchange with a graded dose response. This is
not evidence for a literal Boolean gate, global necessity, a task-general
mechanism, or a universal low-dimensional activation direction.

| Reverse, all positions | 0.9975 | pass |

| Matched attention control, worst absolute mean | 0.0172 | pass |

The one-sided 95% bootstrap upper bound on the forward lexical-versus-all gap
was `0.0063`; in reverse it was `0.0075`. The corresponding endpoint-gap upper
bounds were `0.0050` and `0.0044`. These are far inside the registered `0.03`
limits.

The mean all-position dose curve was:

`0.0000 -> 0.0519 -> 0.5104 -> 0.9390 -> 0.9963`

at doses `0`, `0.25`, `0.50`, `0.75`, and `1`. Ten of twelve individual
contexts had the same complete strict ordering, exactly meeting the registered
context-robustness threshold. The shape is strongly nonlinear, so the result
supports a continuous state rewrite rather than a hard on/off switch.

## Localization details

Patching only the demonstrated answer-token position produced `0.8584` of the
endpoint on average. Demonstration-prefix positions produced `0.2742`, and the
repeated query positions produced `0.4748`. Patching every differing position
except the demonstrated answer produced only `0.0907` signed mean response
(`0.1280` mean absolute response). These interventions are not additive; their
purpose is causal localization, not a decomposition of the total into scalar
shares.

## Why this advances the project

The earlier “hub” result was defined over a whole component update at every
position. E29.14a gives that coarse boundary a substantially clearer meaning:
an early MLP rewrites the content-bearing token states, and later computation
treats those rewritten states almost as if the prompt itself had been changed.
The effect transfers prospectively to unseen words, works in both directions,
is absent at unchanged positions, and is not reproduced by the adjacent
attention component.

The next bridge is to test whether this lexical-position restriction remains
equivalent to all-position patching for fixed large multi-component masks.
Only after that paired bridge passes is it justified to reinterpret the old
c24/c32 component interaction landscape as sparse interactions over lexical
state rather than over whole-sequence component outputs.
